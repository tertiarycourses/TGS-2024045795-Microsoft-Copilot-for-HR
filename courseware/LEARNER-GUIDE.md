Course code: TGS-2024045795

## Course message

Use AI to reduce administrative work, improve productivity and efficiency, and return HR capacity to workforce strategy, coaching, organisation design and employee care. Human professionals remain accountable for consequential decisions and conversations.

## Platform model

- **Microsoft 365 Copilot:** human-directed drafting, summarisation, comparison and analysis.
- **Agent Builder in Microsoft 365 Copilot:** focused team Q&A over approved knowledge.
- **Microsoft Copilot Studio:** bounded agents, tools, flows, channels and lifecycle operations.

## Reference case vignettes

The supplied ebook presents four unnamed secondary-source cases: recruitment screening and candidate chat; retail engagement and retention; financial-services talent development; and healthcare performance coaching. Treat the reported outcomes as discussion evidence, not independently verified company identities.

## Learning Unit 1: From Generative AI to Agentic HR

### Today's HR operating pain

Fragmented channels and repeated hand-offs trap HR capacity in administration.

- Requests arrive through email, forms and chat with no shared case view
- HR rekeys the same data across trackers, documents and systems
- Employees and managers wait for inconsistent answers and approvals
- Evidence, ownership and exceptions are difficult to trace

### The strategic case for HR AI

Automate routine work so HR can deliver faster service and more strategic value.

- Reduce administrative work and avoidable rekeying
- Improve productivity, efficiency and response consistency
- Return capacity to workforce strategy, coaching and employee care
- Use human-AI partnerships to connect the people agenda to business outcomes

Sources: Deloitte, The Future of HR, Powered by AI (user-supplied reference deck); https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/people-and-organizational-performance/our-insights/the-organization-blog/four-ways-to-start-using-generative-ai-in-hr

### Why now: the people and AI agendas converge

HR must build internal capability while helping the enterprise navigate AI disruption.

- 79% of surveyed leaders expected GenAI to substantially transform their organisation or industry
- 72% expected GenAI to change talent strategies
- Only 23% reported GenAI investment in HR in the cited study
- 81% said business and people agendas had never been more intertwined

Sources: Deloitte, The Future of HR, Powered by AI (user-supplied reference deck)

### The HR AI continuum

Automation follows rules; copilots create; agents pursue bounded goals.

- Rules automate predictable hand-offs
- Microsoft 365 Copilot drafts, summarises and analyses
- Agents reason, plan, use tools and act
- Humans retain accountability

Sources: https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/ai-agents-in-human-resources; https://www.hrci.org/community/blogs-and-announcements/hr-leads-business-blog/hr-leads-business/2025/08/21/agentic-ai-in-hr

### Four shifts in HR work

AI value moves from efficiency to new human and enterprise value.

- Automate: eliminate repetitive work about work
- Augment: support people to improve productivity and experience
- Extend: analyse and personalise at a scale humans cannot
- Create: design, test and operate new AI-enabled HR services

Sources: Deloitte, The Future of HR, Powered by AI (user-supplied reference deck)

### HR's own AI evolution

HR moves incrementally from AI-assisted to AI-augmented and AI-powered work.

- AI-assisted: humans own the work with moderate AI support
- AI-augmented: people and AI collaborate with frequent hand-offs
- AI-powered: AI handles bounded work while people monitor and improve
- Fully autonomous HR remains rare; human accountability endures

Sources: Deloitte, The Future of HR, Powered by AI (user-supplied reference deck)

### Conventional HR operating model

Queues and hand-offs make service slow and opaque.

- Requests arrive through email and forms
- Specialists search systems manually
- Approvals cross organisational boundaries
- Evidence is scattered across tools

### Copilot-assisted HR operating model

A human remains the operator while Copilot accelerates knowledge work.

- Ground prompts in approved work content

### Lab 1: Assess Talent Needs with Copilot

Slides 36–44

Scenario: HarbourLight Logistics SG is opening a digitally enabled distribution hub. HR receives demand forecasts, overtime data, vacancy history and a skills inventory in separate files, so workforce decisions are slow and reactive.

Learning goal: Use Microsoft 365 Copilot to turn operational evidence into a transparent hire, redeploy or develop recommendation.

Tools: Excel with Microsoft 365 Copilot; Teams

Detailed procedure:

1. Review the synthetic demand, overtime, vacancy and skills-inventory evidence.
2. Define the business outcomes and time horizon for the new hub.
3. Ask Copilot to summarise demand drivers and cite the supporting cells.
4. Compare required capabilities with the current workforce skills inventory.
5. Create hire, redeploy, automate and develop options with assumptions and trade-offs.
6. Stress-test the preferred option against high- and low-demand scenarios.
7. Record the human-approved talent need, success measure and review date.
8. Pass the approved role outcomes and skills to Lab 2.

Evidence to submit:

- Talent-demand evidence map
- Skills-gap analysis
- Hire-redeploy-develop recommendation

Guardrails:

- Cite the supplied cells and records
- Separate fact from forecast
- Business and HR owners approve the demand hypothesis

Scenario questions:

1. Which assumption most changes the talent need?
2. Where could automation reduce workload without removing accountability?
3. What evidence would trigger a plan revision?

### Lab 2: Draft an Inclusive Job Description with Generative AI

Slides 45–53

Scenario: Lab 1 established a need for a Workforce Operations Analyst. The old JD is task-heavy, contains copied requirements and does not reflect the outcomes or skills needed at the new hub.

Learning goal: Use Word Copilot to draft a skills-based, inclusive and approval-ready job description grounded in the approved talent need.

Tools: Word with Microsoft 365 Copilot; Excel

Detailed procedure:

1. Import the approved role outcomes and skills from Lab 1 or use the supplied fallback brief.
2. Identify obsolete, vague or non-job-related requirements in the legacy JD.
3. Prompt Copilot to draft purpose, outcomes, responsibilities and success measures.
4. Separate essential capabilities from skills that can be developed after hiring.
5. Add working conditions, reporting line and realistic candidate information.
6. Run inclusive-language, accessibility and fair-consideration checks.
7. Ask the hiring manager to validate each criterion and remove unsupported requirements.
8. Convert the final JD criteria into the anchored screening rubric for Lab 3.

Evidence to submit:

- Role-outcome map
- Inclusive job description
- Hiring-manager validation checklist

Guardrails:

- Use job-related criteria only
- Distinguish essential from trainable skills
- Hiring manager and HR approve every requirement

Scenario questions:

1. Which copied requirement would unnecessarily narrow the talent pool?
2. What must the hiring manager verify rather than delegate to Copilot?
3. How does the JD make later screening contestable?

### Lab 3: Screen Resumes with a Fair Evidence Matrix

Slides 54–62

Scenario: Six synthetic candidates apply for the Workforce Operations Analyst role from Lab 2. Recruiters currently scan resumes inconsistently and retype evidence into spreadsheets.

Learning goal: Use Microsoft 365 Copilot to extract job-related evidence at speed while keeping shortlist decisions human, traceable and reviewable.

Tools: Excel with Microsoft 365 Copilot; Word

Detailed procedure:

1. Load the approved JD and anchored rubric from Lab 2 or use the supplied fallback rubric.
2. Review the six synthetic resumes without adding new criteria.
3. Prompt Copilot to extract evidence for each criterion and mark missing information as unknown.
4. Verify every extracted item against the source resume.
5. Calculate rubric results using transparent workbook formulas.
6. Check for proxy variables, inconsistent standards and false negative risk.
7. Record the authorised recruiter's shortlist decision separately from AI output.
8. Pass the shortlisted evidence and unresolved questions to Lab 4.

Evidence to submit:

- Candidate evidence matrix
- Uncertainty and bias log
- Human shortlist rationale

Guardrails:

- Synthetic resumes only
- Protected attributes and proxies excluded
- AI may organise evidence but cannot reject a candidate

Scenario questions:

1. Where could absence of evidence become an unfair negative assumption?
2. How can a candidate challenge or correct the evidence?
3. Which screening error creates the greatest harm?

### Lab 4: Prepare and Review Structured Interviews with AI

Slides 79–87

Scenario: The recruiter shortlisted three candidates in Lab 3. Managers currently improvise interview questions, take incomplete notes and struggle to compare evidence consistently.

Learning goal: Use Copilot to prepare job-related structured questions, organise synthetic interview notes and support a human panel decision.

Tools: Word and Teams with Microsoft 365 Copilot; Excel

Detailed procedure:

1. Review the JD outcomes, rubric and shortlisted evidence from Labs 2–3.
2. Ask Copilot to draft behavioural questions linked to each job outcome.
3. Create anchored indicators for strong, partial and insufficient evidence.
4. Add accessibility, candidate-consent and prohibited-question checks.
5. Run the supplied synthetic interview transcripts or role-play the panel.
6. Use Copilot to organise notes by criterion without inferring intent, truthfulness or personality.
7. Have panel members score independently, then record evidence-based consensus.
8. Draft consistent offer, hold and decline communications for human approval.

Evidence to submit:

- Structured interview guide
- Anchored panel scorecard
- Candidate communication pack

Guardrails:

- Same core questions for every candidate
- No emotion or personality inference
- Panel members score independently before consensus

Scenario questions:

1. Which question best tests a role outcome rather than similarity to the panel?
2. What interview evidence should Copilot never infer?
3. How will disagreements between panel members be resolved?

## Learning Unit 4: Responsible Adoption and HR Transformation

Slides 149–193 · Govern decisions, data, people and agent operations

### Why HR AI is high consequence

Employment decisions affect opportunity, income, dignity and trust.

- Recruitment and promotion shape opportunity
- Performance evidence can be incomplete
- Employee data is sensitive
- Power asymmetry raises consent concerns

Sources: https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/help-and-resources/2020/01/model-ai-governance-framework; https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-practices/fair-consideration-framework

### Human involvement by impact

More consequential and less reversible decisions need stronger human authority.

- Inform: AI drafts or retrieves
- Recommend: AI proposes with evidence
- Approve: authorised human decides
- Execute: agent acts only within approved bounds

Sources: https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/help-and-resources/2020/01/model-ai-governance-framework; https://www.strategyand.pwc.com/de/en/functions/organisational-strategy/agentic-ai-in-hr.html

### From HR efficiency to enterprise value

The strongest human-AI partnerships deliver better workforce solutions, speed and business impact.

- Workforce solutions become more personalised and responsive
- Agile portfolio management reduces time from need to usable service
- Administrative capacity shifts to strategic and business-facing work
- AI and technology fluency becomes a capability across HR, not an HRIS silo

Sources: Deloitte, The Future of HR, Powered by AI (user-supplied reference deck)

### Four implementation case vignettes

The supplied ebook illustrates value across recruitment, engagement, talent and performance.

- Technology: screening and candidate chat; reported 30% faster hiring
- Retail: feedback analysis; reported 20% lower turnover
- Financial services: predictive talent and succession development
- Healthcare: timely evidence for coaching and reviews

Sources: AI Revolution in Human Resources: Transforming HR Management with Artificial Intelligence (2023 user-supplied ebook)

### What successful implementations share

Technology works when objectives, people, monitoring and cross-functional ownership reinforce one another.

- Start with a clear employee or business outcome
- Involve employees early in design, testing and feedback
- Continuously monitor quality, bias, drift and unintended effects
- Pair HR process ownership with IT, legal, security and data expertise

Sources: AI Revolution in Human Resources: Transforming HR Management with Artificial Intelligence (2023 user-supplied ebook)

### Privacy by design

Minimise data before asking a model to protect it.

- Purpose and lawful use
- Minimum necessary fields
- Access, retention and deletion
- Incident and data-subject response

Sources: https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/help-and-resources/2020/01/model-ai-governance-framework

### Fair hiring controls

Selection criteria must remain job-related, reviewable and consistently applied.

- Fairly consider candidates
- Exclude non-job-related protected traits
- Keep interview and decision evidence
- Provide human challenge and correction

Sources: https://www.mom.gov.sg/employment-practices/fair-consideration-framework; https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/help-and-resources/2020/01/model-ai-governance-framework

### Bias is a system property

Bias can enter through objectives, data, prompts, tools and human use.

- Question proxy variables
- Compare outcomes across relevant groups
- Review false positives and negatives
- Change process, not only the prompt

Sources: https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/help-and-resources/2020/01/model-ai-governance-framework; https://www.sap.com/resources/ai-for-hr

### Transparency and explainability

Employees need to know when AI is used and how to seek help.

- Disclose the AI role
- Show source and material factors
- Explain limits and uncertainty
- Provide an accessible human channel

Sources: https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/help-and-resources/2020/01/model-ai-governance-framework

### Security and prompt injection

An agent can be manipulated through the text it reads.

- Treat retrieved content as untrusted
- Separate instructions from data
- Allow-list tools and destinations
- Monitor anomalous actions

Sources: https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/help-and-resources/2020/01/model-ai-governance-framework; https://www.microsoft.com/en-sg/microsoft-365-copilot/microsoft-copilot-studio

### Agent identity and least privilege

Every action should be attributable to an authorised identity.

- Use named service or user context
- Grant only required permissions
- Separate maker, approver and operator roles
- Review access and dormant agents

Sources: https://www.microsoft.com/en-sg/microsoft-365-copilot/microsoft-copilot-studio; https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/ai-agents-in-human-resources

### Operational controls

Agents are digital workers that need owners, logs, limits and retirement.

- Named business and technical owners
- Versioned instructions and knowledge
- Run-time monitoring and kill switch
- Periodic validation and decommissioning

Sources: https://www.hrci.org/community/blogs-and-announcements/hr-leads-business-blog/hr-leads-business/2025/08/21/agentic-ai-in-hr; https://www.strategyand.pwc.com/de/en/functions/organisational-strategy/agentic-ai-in-hr.html

### Success measures

Measure service, adoption, quality, risk and human value together.

- Cycle time and first-contact resolution
- Accuracy and evidence completeness
- Escalation and override rates
- Employee trust and time returned to people

Sources: https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/people-and-organizational-performance/our-insights/the-organization-blog/four-ways-to-start-using-generative-ai-in-hr; https://www.gartner.com/en/human-resources/topics/artificial-intelligence-in-hr

### Change and AI literacy

Adoption depends on role clarity and practical learning.

- Explain what changes and what does not
- Train prompting, review and escalation
- Create champions and feedback loops
- Recognise new work: agent owner, tester, steward

Sources: https://www.gartner.com/en/human-resources/topics/artificial-intelligence-in-hr; https://www.auxis.com/ai-in-hr-examples-types-use-cases-best-practices/

### Build HR's AI capability

HR needs literacy, ethical judgement, product thinking and change leadership to operate AI well.

- Teach AI capabilities, limitations and evidence review
- Establish privacy, fairness and responsible-use guidelines
- Create cross-functional teams for design and sustainment
- Communicate role changes and build continuous learning into work

Sources: AI Revolution in Human Resources: Transforming HR Management with Artificial Intelligence (2023 user-supplied ebook); Deloitte, The Future of HR, Powered by AI (user-supplied reference deck)

### 90-day responsible roadmap

Move from a bounded pilot to an owned capability through evidence gates.

- Days 1–30: baseline, risk and prototype
- Days 31–60: test, controls and user feedback
- Days 61–90: measured pilot and operating handover
- Scale only when value and control thresholds pass

Sources: https://www.strategyand.pwc.com/de/en/functions/organisational-strategy/agentic-ai-in-hr.html; https://www.auxis.com/ai-in-hr-examples-types-use-cases-best-practices/

## Responsible HR AI checklist

- Purpose and scope are explicit.
- Only necessary and authorised data are used.
- Evidence, uncertainty and limitations are visible.
- Consequential decisions have an authorised human approver.
- Normal, edge, adversarial and failure cases are tested.
- Logs, incidents, feedback, changes and retirement have owners.
